CoSpaDi: Compressing LLMs via Calibration-Guided Sparse Dictionary Learning
Authors: Denis Makhov, Dmitriy Shopkhoev, Magauiya Zhussip, Ammar Ali, Stamatios Lefkimmiatis
First: 2025-09-26T08:55:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T17:53:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Post-training LLM compression often relies on low-rank approximations, which force all columns of a projection matrix to share a single low-dimensional subspace. We propose CoSpaDi, a training-free compression framework that replaces this single-subspace assumption with a union-of-subspaces model via sparse dictionary learning. CoSpaDi factorizes each weight matrix into a dense dictionary and column-sparse coefficients, allowing different columns to select different subsets of dictionary atoms at the same storage budget. To preserve model behavior, we use calibration activations to transform functional reconstruction into a standard dictionary learning problem. Across LLaMA and Qwen models, CoSpaDi improves accuracy-compression and perplexity-compression trade-offs over SVD-based and structured pruning baselines at 20-40\% compression ratios, while naturally supporting sparse-dense computation and post-training quantization of sparse coefficients.

Pretraining Knows What, Simulation Teaches How: Compositional Generalization in a Sim-Trained Robot Agent
Authors: Benjamin Pikus, Daniel Saunders, Mircea Mironenco, Salman Mohammadi, Mathew Mathew, Sirou Zhu, Divya Makkar, Xuhua Huang, Qinbo Li, Supreeth Narasimhaswamy, Siyuan Cen, Thomas Neff, Dejia Xu, Shyamal Buch

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A robot policy needs to understand what a task is asking for in a scene, and how to move the robot to accomplish that task. To achieve these two skills, we introduce Zero-real Agent Policy (ZAP), a recipe that teaches a small off-the-shelf vision-language model to control a robot via a simple tool harness. We use a pretrained 27B parameter model to bootstrap world understanding and reasoning, followed by supervised fine-tuning and GRPO on simple simulated tasks to teach it how to move the robot. Without any real-world robot data during training, ZAP reaches 96.7% success on real-world tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in our harness and outperforming the MolmoAct2 VLA (33.3%), while running 3x faster and 17x cheaper than GPT-6 Astra. We demonstrate that ZAP also generalizes to completely new objects, goals, strategies, and language by seating a test tube in a rack (and choosing a different hole in the rack when prompted), handing a test tube between arms mid-air, drawing an X on a tic-tac-toe board, and playing chess. These goals and types of manipulation don't appear in our training data. Furthermore, ZAP also transfers to an unseen embodiment. On a single-arm Franka, ZAP reaches 55.9% on RoboLab-120, 4.9 percentage points ahead of the leading VLA/WAM baseline.

VidEdit3D: Flexible 3D Gaussian Editing with Video Diffusion
Authors: Yanming Yang, Chenxi Song, Tong Zhao, Juntao Xu, Wenhao Rao, Yiwen Chen, Yiwei Wang, Chi Zhang
First: 2026-10-26T17:03:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T17:03:44+00:00
Comments: Project Page: https://2hitee.github.io/VidEdit3D-page/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Recent advancements in text-guided 3D editing, particularly with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have shown promise but still lag behind the flexibility of 2D editing tools. Existing methods face a critical trade-off between preserving multi-view consistency and allowing structural modifications. Image-based editors can also struggle to apply the intended edit to objects viewed from unusual angles. To address these limitations, we introduce VidEdit3D, a framework that reformulates 3D scene editing as a trajectory-based video editing task. By drawing a parallel between camera movement in static 3D scenes and temporal dynamics in video, we leverage the robust spatiotemporal priors of pre-trained Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) to encourage semantic and structural coherence across views. Specifically, we propose Trajectory-Guided Rendering (TGR) to select informative keyframes and construct smooth video clips aligned with VDM input requirements. To propagate edits from an anchor view to these clips, we develop Flow-Aligned Video Editing (FAVE), a training-free framework incorporating an Editing Consistency Guidance (ECG) mechanism. ECG contrasts source and edited image conditions at a shared latent state, using scheduled source-image amplification to strengthen adherence to the edited reference while maintaining original scene context. Finally, we introduce Region-Selective 3DGS Refinement (RS-GS) to strictly confine geometric and appearance updates to target primitives, reducing background attribute leakage. Experiments demonstrate stronger instruction alignment and better multi-view consistency than the baselines.

Triage: Skip, Skim, or Refine Attention Tiles in Video Diffusion Transformers
Authors: Yeonjae Kim, Omin Kwon, Seong Hoon Seo, Jae W. Lee
First: 2026-10-26T16:29:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T16:29:36+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Attention over tens of thousands of visual tokens dominates the inference of video diffusion transformers (DiTs). Existing methods reduce this cost through sparsity, low precision, or both, but use a single precision for all kept tiles. We observe that the output error introduced by approximation varies widely across tiles. Many tiles can be skipped with little error, others must be computed but gain little beyond low precision, and a few retain substantial error even at low precision. We present Triage, a training-free attention method that assigns each tile one of three approximation tiers: skipping it by replacing it with its block means, skimming it in NVFP4, or refining it with NVFP4 residual correction. A lightweight router predicts each tile's error under each tier from block statistics and moves a tile to a more accurate tier only when the predicted error reduction justifies the extra compute, with a single price setting this trade-off. A fused kernel executes all three tiers in a single pass on the FP4 tensor cores of RTX Blackwell GPUs. Across Wan2.2, HunyuanVideo, and LTX-2.5, Triage accelerates end-to-end generation by up to 2.32x over dense FlashAttention, and in each of five settings it runs faster than every baseline while reaching 0.3-3.0 dB higher PSNR than the highest-PSNR baseline.

Explainable Multimodal AI Approach to Reduce Unnecessary Breast Biopsies
Authors: Sadam Hussain, Mansoor Ali, Daly Betzabeth Avendaño-Avalos, Jose Gerardo Tamez-Peña
First: 2026-10-26T16:29:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T16:29:09+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recently, there has been rise in multimodal vision-language models for computer-aided diagnosis (CAD), yet their application in breast diagnosis, particularly for predicting biopsy outcomes remains limited. This study presents a multimodal vision-language approach, incorporating Grad-CAM as an Explainable AI (XAI) technique, to predict benign tumors in patients recommended for biopsy, especially those classified as BI-RADS 4 and 5. A novel multimodal framework was devel- oped using Vision Transformer (ViT) for imaging feature extraction and DeBERTa-V3-Large for textual embeddings. The model was trained and evaluated on a comprehensive in-house dataset comprising 1,632 patients, each with four mammography views and a corresponding radiology report. The features of both modalities were extracted independently and fused by late fusion before classification. Grad-CAM was used to generate visual explanations highlighting key regions influencing model predictions. The proposed model demonstrated high sensitivity 90%, 95%, 99%, and 100% with corresponding specificities of 40%, 36%, 18%, and 11%, respectively. The best AUC achieved was 0.72 by multimodal approach on the test set, outperforming the image only (0.66) and text-only (0.68) baselines. The proposed multimodal architecture, enhanced with Grad-CAM, demonstrates a strong potential to identify benign tumors among high-risk patients, offering interpretable insights and thereby helping reduce unnecessary biopsies, patient anxiety, and healthcare costs.

TrySee: Modular Perception-Enhanced VLM Grounding for Closed-Loop Robot Task Planning in Specialized Domains
Authors: Elias Hofele, Thomas Trapp, David Timmermann, Tristan Schnell, Georg Heppner, Arne Roennau
First: 2026-10-26T16:20:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T16:20:28+00:00
Comments: To be published in the proceedings of the 2026 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Biomimetics (ROBIO)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision Language Models (VLMs) have shown impressive capabilities in high-level reasoning and task planning for robots. However, specialized domains remain a challenge for VLMs' image interpretation and grounding abilities. We introduce TrySee, a modular closed-loop robot task planning pipeline integrating general-purpose VLM reasoning with domain-specific perception and established robotics components. The system consists of an inherently reactive cyclic planning pipeline, augmented with domain-specific modular vision preprocessing, to provide online grounding. Finally, a dynamic preprocessing selection mechanism contributes to the scalability of the pipeline. The system requires neither VLM fine-tuning nor end-to-end demonstrations and adapts through replaceable perception modules and motion planning. The approach is evaluated on planning tasks across 5 widely different domains, including a real-world robot disassembly task. The field tests in a brownfield environment demonstrate the approach's practicality in integrating VLM planning into specialized robotic systems with limited domain-specific training data.

Diversifying Long Prompt Image Generation through Structured Prompt Embedding Space Sampling
Authors: Bo-Kai Ruan, Teng-Fang Hsiao, Ling Lo, Yi-Lun Wu, Hong-Han Shuai
First: 2025-11-25T12:25:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:37:39+00:00
Comments: Accepted by BMVC 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Modern text-to-image models produce impressive visual results from richly specified prompts, yet their behavior under long prompts remains insufficiently understood. In this paper, we study a practical failure mode in which accumulated semantic constraints progressively suppress output variation, causing diversity to collapse even when many visual factors remain unspecified. We show that this phenomenon appears consistently across recent generation models as prompt length increases, and provide a theoretical motivation that connects long-prompt conditioning with reduced sampling entropy in the prompt embedding space. Based on this observation, we introduce PromptMoG, a training-free approach that samples prompt embeddings from a Mixture-of-Gaussians distribution to restore generative flexibility while maintaining semantic fidelity. To support systematic evaluation, we further present LPD-Bench, a structured benchmark of long and semantically dense prompts for measuring both fidelity and diversity under compositional text conditioning. Extensive experiments on four large-scale diffusion models, including SD3.5-Large, Flux.1-Krea-Dev, CogView4, and Qwen-Image, show that PromptMoG consistently improves diversity for long-prompt image generation. The code is publicly available at https://github.com/basiclab/PromptMoG.

标题：通过结构化提示嵌入空间采样实现长提示图像生成的多样化
现代文本到图像模型能够从丰富指定的提示中生成令人印象深刻的视觉结果，但它们在长提示下的行为仍然不够理解。在本文中，我们研究了一种实际的失败模式，在这种模式中，累积的语义约束逐渐抑制输出的多样性，即使许多视觉因素仍然未指定，多样性也会崩溃。我们展示了这种现象在最近的生成模型中随着提示长度增加而一致出现，并提供了一个理论动机，将长提示条件与提示嵌入空间中减少的采样熵联系起来。基于这一观察，我们引入了PromptMoG，这是一种无需训练的方法，通过从混合高斯分布中采样提示嵌入来恢复生成灵活性，同时保持语义保真度。为了支持系统的评估，我们还提出了LPD-Bench，这是一个结构化的长且语义密集的提示基准，用于在组合文本条件下的保真度和多样性测量。在四个大规模扩散模型上的广泛实验，包括SD3.5-Large、Flux.1-Krea-Dev、CogView4和Qwen-Image，表明PromptMoG在长提示图像生成中始终如一地提高了多样性。代码可在https://github.com/basiclab/PromptMoG公开获取。
Summary / 总结
This paper addresses the issue of reduced diversity in image generation from long prompts, which can cause semantic constraints to suppress output variation. The authors introduce PromptMoG, a training-free method that samples prompt embeddings from a Mixture-of-Gaussians distribution to restore generative flexibility while preserving semantic fidelity. Experiments on four large-scale diffusion models demonstrate that PromptMoG effectively improves diversity in long-prompt image generation.
本文研究了现代文本到图像模型在长提示下生成图像时多样性减少的问题。提出了一种无需训练的方法PromptMoG，通过从混合高斯分布中采样提示嵌入来增强生成的灵活性，同时保持语义的准确性。实验表明，PromptMoG在四个大规模扩散模型上一致地提高了长提示图像生成的多样性。
