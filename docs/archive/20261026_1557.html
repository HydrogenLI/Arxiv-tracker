MiD-VLM: Detecting and Grounding Global Semantic Misinformation in Image-Caption Pairs
Authors: Tarunya Prasad, Bharatesh Chakravarthi
First: 2026-10-23T17:55:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T17:55:02+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Semantic misinformation can arise when an authentic image is paired with a caption that changes or distorts its narrative. Because the image itself may contain no visible signs of manipulation, methods designed to detect pixel-level artifacts can miss these misleading image-caption pairs. We study this problem using DGM4+, a dataset designed to evaluate the detection of global scene and semantic inconsistencies. We propose a multimodal model that combines image-text representations from SigLIP with visual scene features from DINOv2 through a projection MLP, and adapts Qwen2-VL-7B using LoRA. The model jointly predicts whether an image-caption pair is authentic, identifies the manipulation type, and grounds the misleading caption tokens. On the DGM4+ validation split, the model achieves an F1 score of $0.9679$ for binary authenticity classification. These results suggest that parameter-efficient adaptation of vision-language models is promising for detecting semantic inconsistencies, while also motivating further work on precise token-level grounding.

Abstract
Objective: To develop a unified vision-language model for prompt-conditioned quantitative ultrasound measurement that addresses the mismatch between continuous measurement targets and discrete token-based numerical prediction. Methods: This article presents HAD-VLM, a hybrid autoregressive-diffusion framework that combines autoregressive text generation with continuous measurement prediction within a shared backbone. Visual prompt tuning adapts the vision encoder to ultrasound images. A diffusion process predicts a five-component representation comprising caliper endpoint coordinates and measurement length. A post-processing strategy combining the proposed coordinate-length consistency mechanism with ensemble prediction is introduced to refine measurement estimates. We evaluate four echocardiographic measurement tasks on EchoNet-LVH, with additional assessments of medical question answering and X-ray vertebral measurement. Results: HAD-VLM achieves mean absolute errors of 1.36, 2.77, 3.05, and 1.16 mm for IVSd, LVIDd, LVIDs, and LVPWd, respectively, reducing errors by 21.4-37.6\% relative to the Vicuna-based autoregressive baseline. It also outperforms fine-tuned Qwen3-VL and direct MLP regression across all four tasks. Medical question answering performance is preserved on the evaluated dataset. For X-ray vertebral measurement, HAD-VLM reduces mean absolute error from 11.4 to 6.7 pixels. Conclusion: Integrating autoregressive language generation with diffusion-based continuous prediction improves quantitative medical image measurement while retaining prompt-conditioned task flexibility. Significance: HAD-VLM provides a unified approach to combining language interaction with anatomically grounded numerical prediction for quantitative medical image analysis.

Privacy Accounting for Empirical Denoisers

Comments: 31 pages, 7 figures

Abstract
Diffusion models have emerged as the foundation of modern generative systems, yet their high memorization capacity raises privacy concerns. While differentially private (DP) training provides formal guarantees, it comes at a cost in sample quality. In this work, we study a complementary question: how much privacy loss is incurred during the sampling process itself? Directly analyzing modern neural denoisers is challenging because their sensitivity to a single training example is generally intractable. To overcome this challenge, we construct and analyze a clipped privacy-accountable empirical denoiser, a training-free estimator of the Bayes-optimal denoiser, whose per-step sensitivity can be computed explicitly, allowing each denoising step to be a Gaussian mechanism. This perspective enables privacy accounting under Gaussian DP when each training example contributes to each denoising step, and under $f$-DP trade-off functions when each generated sample uses a single randomly subsampled block of the training data. We report every $(\varepsilon, δ)$ guarantee as a certified upper bound from a privacy loss distribution accountant, and use a cumulant-based Edgeworth approximation as a fast diagnostic, characterizing where it is reliable and where it becomes anti-conservative. We also analyze the privacy loss along the denoising trajectory and show that it grows steeply near the critical window in which semantic content is formed. This observation motivates hybrid samplers that use the private empirical denoiser through the formative steps where it remains a faithful proxy for the neural denoiser, then switch to a public neural denoiser for the remaining refinement. Our results on CIFAR-10 and CelebA-64 show that the privacy of diffusion models depends not only on the training procedure, but also on sampling-time design choices such as subsampling, stopping time, and hybrid denoising.

Abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models inherit rich semantic representations from pretrained Vision-Language Models, yet fine-tuning on limited robot demonstrations degrades this structure and undermines generalization. A fundamental question therefore arises: what constitutes a good action representation? Inspired by the mirror neuron theory's insight that observation and execution share an intention-level encoding, we examine whether a robot's action representations preserve the semantic structure captured by pretrained encoders. Systematic probing confirms that this structure erodes during finetuning, and that its quality synchronizes with both task success and out-of-distribution generalization. We further introduce a plug-and-play method that anchors action representations to a semantic manifold while decomposing representations into a shared semantic channel and a private channel, all discarded at inference, leaving the deployed model unchanged. Validated on different VLA backbones across simulation and real-world benchmarks, our method yields up to +18.7% on real-world in-distribution tasks and +21.5% on out-of-distribution generalization.

Abstract
Echocardiography is the cornerstone of cardiovascular diagnostics, yet its global utility is constrained by a critical shortage of experienced interpreters. Current artificial intelligence (AI) solutions remain limited, typically operating as opaque "black boxes" or focusing on narrow tasks that fail to capture the holistic, guideline-driven reasoning of a clinician. Here we introduce EchoChat, a generative multimodal vision-language model that performs comprehensive echocardiographic analysis to support clinician interpretation. By emulating the cognitive workflow of a cardiologist, EchoChat integrates vast medical knowledge with a chain-of-thought reasoning mechanism to explicitly ground its conclusions in visual evidence. Trained on echocardiography-instruction pairs from 46,358 examinations, our model establishes a new state-of-the-art across diverse cohorts from six international medical centers. It supports the broadest diagnostic scope of 33 cardiac conditions and findings, including 15 newly added conditions for which it achieves an average F1 of 73.7% across available external evaluations. It surpasses existing best-performing foundation models in cardiac condition recognition (improving average recall by 13.54% on shared tasks) and quantification (average R2 score increase of 0.275 on shared tasks). Furthermore, in a source-blinded echocardiographer reader study, echocardiography reports co-generated with EchoChat were rated higher in diagnostic accuracy and completeness by two senior echocardiographers, and improved diagnostic performance from 72.78% to 82.75% in terms of F1 score over routine-reader only reports. These results demonstrate that integrating clinical reasoning into multimodal AI can democratize specialist expertise and transform the standard of cardiovascular care.

Attention-space Contrastive Guidance for Efficient Hallucination Mitigation in LVLMs
Authors: Yujin Jo, Sangyoon Bae, Taesup Kim
Venue: CVPR 2026
First: 2026-01-20T08:04:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T16:39:00+00:00
Comments: Accepted at CVPR 2026 Findings. Code: https://github.com/ZoYujin/ACG

Abstract
Hallucinations in large vision--language models (LVLMs) often arise when language priors dominate over visual evidence, leading to object misidentification and visually inconsistent descriptions. We address this problem by framing hallucination mitigation as contrastive guidance that steers generation toward visually grounded and semantically faithful text. We propose Attention-space Contrastive Guidance (ACG), a training-free, single-pass method that operates directly in self-attention layers, where hallucination-inducing cross-modal biases emerge. ACG constructs both image-conditioned and approximate text-only attention paths within a single forward pass, enabling efficient guidance before errors accumulate at the output layer. Because this masking-based surrogate can introduce approximation bias, we further apply a lightweight orthogonal projection that suppresses components aligned with the text-only path, yielding a more visually grounded correction. Experiments on CHAIR and POPE show that ACG improves faithfulness over existing training-free baselines while maintaining caption quality, reducing latency by up to $2\times$ compared to multi-pass contrastive decoding methods.

From Frames to Temporal Graphs: In-Context Egocentric Action Recognition with Vision-Language Models
Authors: Bessie Dominguez-Dager, Francisco Gomez-Donoso, Miguel Cazorla, Marc Pollefeys, Daniel Barath, Zuria Bauer

Abstract
Action reasoning in egocentric video requires capturing fine-grained transitions of hand-object interactions, a task where general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often struggle when operating directly on raw pixels. We propose to decouple visual perception from symbolic reasoning by converting videos into Temporal Action Graphs. In a multi-stage prompting pipeline, we first generate dense natural language narratives over short temporal windows as a semantic bottleneck, then formalize them into structured, open-vocabulary graph representations. On the EGTEA and Epic-Kitchens-100 datasets, the symbolic representation unlocks efficient in-context learning: few-shot graph demonstrations yield substantial accuracy gains over zero-shot frame-based inference. Even in the zero-shot setting, graph-based reasoning remains competitive with pixel-based inference despite potential pretraining contamination favoring the latter. Across 11 open-weight VLMs from 6 model families ranging from 2B to 235B parameters, our findings indicate that current VLMs are more effective as symbolic reasoners than as direct visual observers. By projecting video into the language domain, we provide a scalable, fine-tuning-free alternative to end-to-end approaches that better leverages these models' latent reasoning strengths. Abstract

Abstract
Local 3D asset editing requires modifying a target region while preserving the rest. Existing methods enforce locality through mechanisms external to the generator, such as manual 3D masks, post-hoc voxel merging, or 2D multi-view lifting. None intervene where the corruption originates: inside the ODE sampler. For faithful local editing, a rectified-flow generator's velocity field should be strong over the target region while vanishing on preserved content. Yet a single velocity field can hardly satisfy both requirements, leading to three problems: (i) identity leakage that keeps the edit signal non-zero on preserved regions; (ii) no dedicated edit-amplification channel, so strengthening the edit perturbs identity; and (iii) identity drag at the geometry and material stages, where a global condition pulls every token toward the target. We propose VS3D (Velocity-Space 3D Asset editing), an inversion-free, training-free, and mask-free framework that derives edit and preserve signals by comparing the frozen model's source- and target-conditioned responses and realizes this principle through three stage-specific modules. Reconstruction-Anchored Source Injection (RASI) absorbs identity leakage by turning the unconditional embedding into a per-step, asset-specific anchor calibrated through source reconstruction. Partial-Mean Guidance (PMG) amplifies the edit signal by contrasting high- and low-quality subsample estimates of the velocity difference, active only where a consistent edit exists. Twin-Agreement Residual Injection (TAR) lets the sampler decide token by token what to preserve at the geometry and material stages. Experiments on diverse 3D assets show that VS3D outperforms state-of-the-art native-3D and 2D-lifted editors, demonstrating the effectiveness of velocity-space editing for training-free 3D asset editing. Code is publicly available at https://github.com/LHaoooo/VS3D.

Abstract
Active 3D Gaussian reconstruction fundamentally relies on selecting informative next-best views under limited sensing budgets. Abstract
Visual grounding with vision-language models such as CLIP typically produces a single deterministic relevance map for an image-query pair, without indicating whether the localisation is reliable or whether the referent is present at all. We introduce \textbf{PROVE} (\textbf{Pro}babilistic \textbf{V}isual Grounding via Patch-Level \textbf{E}vidence), a post-hoc evidential adapter that maps frozen CLIP patch features to per-patch Beta distributions without modifying the backbone. PROVE casts grounding as per-patch membership: each patch accumulates positive and negative evidence for whether it belongs to the referent, so a query may match zero, one, or many patches. We train PROVE with an evidential objective on soft patch-coverage targets, and define a prior-pull objective that keeps absent-referent examples near an uninformative prior, so that vacuity serves as an absence signal without a dedicated presence classifier. During inference, a single forward pass yields, in closed form, a posterior-mean grounding map, a spatial vacuity map, and an image-level absence score. We evaluate PROVE on referring-expression benchmarks: it is competitive in localisation with state-of-the-art CLIP grounding methods, its uncertainty tracks localisation error and supports selective prediction under abstention, and its image-level score detects when the referent is absent altogether. Our evidential patch-membership modelling offers a simple, drop-in mechanism for probabilistic verification in frozen vision-language grounding systems.

Abstract
A failed vision-language-action (VLA) rollout does not imply that every action contributed to failure. Yet in group-relative policy optimization (GRPO), a single trajectory-level advantage is assigned to all actions in a rollout. Potentially useful recovery attempts can thus be penalized alongside the errors they aim to correct. Ideal stopping conditions for failure feedback are characterized using success-probability and policy-score martingales. At these stops, the removed gradient contribution has zero conditional expectation given the retained information. Truncation therefore preserves the expectation of a reference GRPO group-gradient estimator and can reduce its covariance. One such stop occurs when the conditional probability of success is zero, motivating error and recovery cues for selecting candidate feedback boundaries. Martingale GRPO (MART-GRPO) is introduced with a vision-language model (VLM) for event-guided truncation. The VLM is fine-tuned on our annotated training set using low-rank adaptation (LoRA). The last visible error without observed recovery is localized by the frozen VLM in complete rollout videos. Failure feedback is retained through the selected error interval and removed afterward, while successful-rollout loss terms and original loss normalization are preserved. For retrospective boundaries, changes in expectation and variance are analyzed to assess departures from ideal stopping. Experiments with OpenVLA-OFT on RoboTwin 2.0 show that MART-GRPO improves success rates over full-trajectory GRPO across multiple tasks.

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) process images and text jointly, yet robustness evaluations often perturb these inputs separately or assess only predefined corruption levels. We present MoST, a black-box testing framework that characterizes modality sensitivity by comparing image-only, text-only, and joint perturbations. Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) has substantially improved the reasoning capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs), yet it can induce chain-of-thought (CoT) obfuscation, an operational, non-intentional regime where models achieve high accuracy while producing reasoning traces that are less grounded and harder to monitor. While prior work documents this decay at the behavioral level, the underlying representational correlates of such drift remain poorly understood. In this work, we find that obfuscation is accompanied by a representational shift, where RL-induced optimization makes task-agnostic template features less separable from visually grounded content in the model's activation space. Based on this insight, we propose TAME, an activation-level intervention framework that leverages Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to regularize VLM internal features during RL training. Specifically, TAME utilizes an anti-obfuscation feedback loop to reduce behavioral obfuscation and applies an asymmetric SAE constraint to specific internal directions, penalizing template activations while preserving visual evidence. Experiments on the VIRL-39k and SPA-VL benchmarks across two model families show that TAME improves CoT monitorability (by up to +30.9 points on VIRL and +16.7 points on SPA-VL over GRPO), while largely preserving task accuracy and general-capability performance across four standard VLM benchmarks, with task-specific trade-offs. Abstract
Deep generative models that can tractably compute input likelihoods, including normalizing flows, often assign unexpectedly high likelihoods to out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs. We mitigate this paradox from an entropic perspective with Semantic Proportional Entropy Manipulation (SPEM), a training-free procedure that applies stronger perturbations to inputs less similar to an in-distribution (ID) memory bank. We derive theoretical lower bounds showing that this entropy gap increases the expected log-likelihood difference in favor of the ID, and experiments on standard benchmarks show that this manipulation realigns the likelihood ordering with human intuition. Strikingly, this realignment persists even when the flow is given only visually meaningless noise instead of the original input, and we analyze this phenomenon theoretically to characterize the conditions under which it occurs.

Abstract
Self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViTs) like DINO encode rich semantics, enabling training-free segmentation of objects from the background and discovery of their parts via feature clustering. This paper focuses on part discovery: partitioning a foreground object into semantic parts. Existing approaches produce imprecise part boundaries. We trace this to the rigid ViT patch grid used in training DINO, where patches straddling boundaries contain pixels from multiple parts. While DINO's invariance-based training could absorb this pixel-level mixing into clean features, experiments with over 26K foreground patches show it does not: boundary-overlapping patches produce features with higher entropy and lower assignment confidence, blurring predicted part boundaries. Rather than make the massive effort of retraining DINO over geometrically coherent regions, an easier approach is to take boundaries directly from a boundary detector, which supplies the geometric information DINO's features lack. Motivated by this, we introduce Part-DINO, a training-free framework that combines boundaries from low-level image segmentation with DINO's semantic features. We use the boundaries to partition the image into regions, pool DINO features within each region, and group them to discover parts. Without any training, Part-DINO produces structurally coherent part assignments, outperforming training-free baselines across four standard benchmarks (CUB, CelebA, PartImageNet-OOD, PartImageNet-Seg) and remaining competitive with weakly-supervised and unsupervised methods.
